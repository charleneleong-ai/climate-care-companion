Architecture reference

Seven layers from a postcode to a caregiver knowing what to do tonight. Layers 0 to 4 are the whole product; 5 and 6 make it sellable.

01

The stack

Each layer consumes the one below it and adds exactly one thing. Select a layer for its sources, owner and tables.

02

Three rules holding it together

L3 has no I/O

A pure function. Testable, demoable with the network down, and replayable against last July's real weather.

Everything ends in an action

A risk tier with no next step is a weather app. Every reason code maps to something the caregiver can do.

L6 is a query, not a system

Council and hospital views are aggregations over the same assessment table. Hours of work, not weeks.

03

Escalation ladder

Each rung asks whether the situation can be resolved here. Yes, stop. No, climb.

04

Who the alert reaches

The official cascade runs UKHSA to NHS England to integrated care boards to providers, with a parallel branch to local authorities and local resilience forums. Every link joins one organisation to another.

TierScaleDutyGets the alert
Where it stops. Last summer 677 heat deaths were in care homes and 485 in hospitals — both inside the cascade. 326 were in people's own homes, which is outside it, and that figure was not statistically significant, meaning under-detected rather than absent. ONS holds no official count of unpaid carers because they are unregistered, so the cascade cannot reach them even in principle.
05

Build order

Core first, against hardcoded exposure and seeded personas. Get a real tier with a real reasons list printing to the terminal before touching a network call.
